> 1. A screen recording captured on a physical device, running the latest operating system, demonstrating the app's functionality. The recording must begin with launching the app and show the typical user flow.

The recording is attached. It was captured on a physical device running the latest operating system and shows app launch and the main features. No account, login, or paid features. Users can share personal maps; there is no public feed or chat.

> 2. A description of the app's purpose and target audience, including the problem it solves and the value it provides

JapanEx helps travelers record their experiences across Japan’s 47 prefectures and see their travel progress on a map.

> 3. Instructions for setting up and accessing the app's main features, including any required login credentials or sample files

No setup, credentials, or sample files are needed. Tap a prefecture on Map to set its level. Use Flags to search and filter, and Settings to change language. Map also supports image and link sharing.

> 4. A list of the external services, tools, or platforms the app uses to deliver its core functionality (for example, data providers, authentication services, payment processors, or AI services)

Core features work offline. The app uses Expo/React Native, local SQLite storage, the iOS share sheet, and Expo hosting for shared links. No authentication, payment, AI, advertising, or analytics services.

> 5. Describe any regional differences in the app’s features or content, or confirm that the app functions consistently across all regions

Features and content are the same across regions. English, Japanese, and Traditional Chinese are supported.

> 6. If the app operates in a highly regulated industry or includes protected third-party material, provide any relevant documentation or credentials to demonstrate you are authorized to provide these services or protected material

No regulated services. The map is adapted from the MIT-licensed JapanEx project. Flag images are from Wikimedia Commons: Hiroshima and Kagawa are CC BY-SA 3.0; the others are marked public domain. Sources and licenses are listed in the app and the attached THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md.
